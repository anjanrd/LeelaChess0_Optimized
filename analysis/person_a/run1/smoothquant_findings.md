# SmoothQuant mechanism and observed outliers

Across all 101 saved groups, 100 have a lower max/median ratio; 1 do not. All groups are retained in the CSV and F12. The group summary records original-build channel statistics and an algebraic rescaling, not fresh inference. The separate 768-channel selected-model example was collected on CPU from both sides of the same Mul using the unchanged 512 calibration inputs. It satisfies after = before × smooth_inverse at the original rtol=1e-5, atol=1e-12; maximum observed absolute difference is zero. Its peak/median channel-maximum ratio falls from 676,662.01 to 1,131.17; all 768 channels are live. A channel maximum is the largest absolute value seen for that channel across all calibration inputs and board tokens. The ratio compares the largest channel maximum with the median channel maximum. It is neither a WDL probability nor accuracy.

The original failed check mixed model versions: the old saved arrays belong to C1 (alpha 0.5, percentile 99.99), verified by matching collection-log/backup hashes and all 768 scaling values. They are preserved under reports/, together with the original failure record. F12 now uses the final C2 (alpha 0.7, percentile 99.999) pair in analysis/person_a/a5/current-channel-maxima.npz. The original FP32 maxima independently agree with the final LN sidecar-derived ratios at rtol=1e-6; they are not substituted for the measured within-s4 before array. See a5/provenance.json and a5/collection.json.

For a positive channel scale s, XW = (X/s)(sW) before quantization. This implementation inserts a Mul on the selected branches and compensates their weights. Unrestricted LayerNorm folding would also affect residual consumers. Shared global Smolgen weights are not independently smoothed per block. The chosen alpha is 0.7 from development selection; test plots are explanation, not another tuning pass. The better printed policy/value fidelity supports the technique here; no latency claim follows from this plot.
