2:4 sparsity: pruning in the pattern the GPU can skip
T H E  P A T T E R N
0.8 0 -0.3 0 0 1.1 0 0.4
0 -0.6 0 0.2 0.9 0 -0.2 0
Two weight rows, split into groups of four
• In every group of four weights, exactly two are zero.
• The sparse tensor cores on the RTX 4060 skip the zeros, which halves the 
multiplications on paper.
• End to end we expect far less than 2x, because only the matmuls get 
faster.

Does search cope better with policy error or value error?
FP16 network
exact P and V

noisy P and V
S W A P  1
INT8 policy + FP16 value
Only the policy is noisy
S W A P  2
FP16 policy + INT8 value
Only the value is noisy
Both mixes go into the same fixed-node search and are scored against FP16 and full INT8. Two 
networks per position make timing meaningless, so this runs at fixed nodes only.
W H Y  N O T  K E E P  O N E  H E A D  I N  F P 1 6 ?
Almost all of BT3's compute is in the shared body. The policy and value 
heads are small layers on top of it. Keeping one head in FP16 still 
leaves it reading noisy INT8 features, so both variants would look like 
full INT8. Swapping the outputs of two whole networks isolates each 
error cleanly.
H O W  W E  R E A D  T H E  R E S U L T
• If swap 1 stays close to FP16 and swap 2 drops, the search absorbs 
policy error but not value error.
• A plausible reason: search explores past a bad policy, but a wrong 
value is backed up the tree. We will test this, not assume it.
This tells us which part of the INT8 error the search can repair.
